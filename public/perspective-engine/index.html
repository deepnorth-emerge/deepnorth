The Perspective Engine

You'll be given a dilemma and asked to choose. Then we'll look at what happened while you chose.

About three minutes. Nothing is scored, sent or saved.

Which way would you go?

Go with your first answer.

You chose .

What was there in the moment you chose?

The other answer was also true.

So was yours. This was never the kind of question that has an answer. It has two things that are both right, pulling against each other, and people you care about on each side.

Here's what happened in the few seconds you spent on it.

  • The sortA live situation with people in it became two folders, and you picked one. The page offered two buttons, which helped, but most rooms offer two buttons too.
  • The close
  • The unease underneathOpen questions register as threat. Choosing is the fastest way to make the alarm go quiet, whether or not the choice is any good.

None of this is a mistake. It's the mind most of us run on, doing what it was built to do. The question is whether it's the only thing available.

Same dilemma. This time you don't choose.

Keep both on the screen. Stay with them until the line reaches the edge.

What do you notice now that you didn't when you were choosing?

That was holding.

It's slower than choosing and less comfortable. It also lets in what the sort throws away: the other side's reasons, the people in the middle, the parts nobody knows yet. Most people find more there than they expected, including options that weren't on either button.

We aren't short of intelligence. We're short of the capacity to hold what our intelligence keeps sorting. That gap, and the low hum of unease it leaves in so many of us, is what Cognitive Unease is about.
